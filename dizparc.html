UX Design 2025 Research · Workshop · Process Mapping

Dizparc

Designing a clearer way to manage project documentation and team collaboration

The challenge

Project documentation is a complex process with many actors, documents and decisions. The main pain points were poor communication, unclear responsibilities and no central place to gather information.

My role

I worked in the project team to map the documentation process, facilitate workshop sessions and turn user needs into a clearer digital workflow.

Key outcome

A platform concept that brings the process together in one place, with visibility, shared documentation and clearer ownership across the team.

Tools

Workshops, process mapping, research synthesis, concept development, prototyping

The process

From complexity to clarity

01

Research and workshop mapping

We started by mapping how project documentation work actually unfolds in practice. Through a workshop with stakeholders, we gathered their individual processes and then merged them into a shared understanding of the whole workflow.

The biggest finding was that people did not struggle because they lacked tools alone they struggled because the process was fragmented. Information was spread across people, files and responsibilities, which created delays and confusion.

02

Identifying the core needs

From the workshop and research synthesis, we identified recurring needs: easier communication between actors, clearer project overview, shared access to documents, and improved visibility about who was responsible for what.

The findings also showed that a lack of shared structure was a key issue. Teams needed a place where they could follow the process, find previous work and stay aligned without relying on one person to hold everything together.

03

Concept development

We translated the findings into a concept for a project documentation platform designed to create calm in an otherwise complex process. The idea was to gather the entire workflow in one place: process overview, documents, meetings, responsibilities and team communication.

We started with a feature overview and information architecture, then sketched key screens in lo-fi before moving to higher fidelity.

04

Design direction

We deliberately aimed for a cleaner and calmer interface instead of adding more complexity. The design was built around trust and clarity, so users could easily understand the current step, see what was still pending and find the right person to contact.

The final solution focused on making the process feel more manageable, not on adding more functions for the sake of it.

Outcome

What I delivered

A concept for a project documentation platform that gathers key information in one place and makes responsibilities, process steps and documentation easier to understand. The design work focused on reducing uncertainty and helping teams work with greater confidence.

Shared overview of the project documentation process

Improved communication between project actors

Centralised document and responsibility tracking

Archive of previous project documentation to reduce repeated work

Reflection

What I learned

The key lesson from Dizparc was that the best design solutions often come from understanding the real process before solving the problem. In complex documentation work, the challenge is not only the volume of information it is fragmentation. When communication, responsibilities and documents are all spread out, the experience becomes slow and unclear. A better tool should reduce that friction instead of adding more layers.
