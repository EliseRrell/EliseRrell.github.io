Eleiko

Designing a circular service model for premium gym equipment

The challenge

Eleiko sells premium equipment with a long lifespan, but the company had no clear strategy for what happened after the equipment was sold. That left a large second-hand market outside their control and created a circularity gap.

My role

I worked in the project team with a focus on stakeholder research, customer insights and service concept development for a circular business model.

Key outcome

Two concept directions: a rental and leasing model, and a buy-back / circular trade-in concept that would give Eleiko more control over equipment at end-of-life.

Tools

Interviews, stakeholder analysis, journey mapping, service blueprinting, concept development

The process

From linear sales to circular service

01

Research and stakeholder mapping

The project began with a broad research phase. We looked at Eleiko's current business model, brand values and the market context, then conducted interviews with hotels, gyms and property owners to understand how customers buy, use and eventually replace gym equipment.

We identified a clear pattern: the biggest pain points were high purchase cost, uncertainty around long-term value, and the lack of a structured way to handle used equipment. The research showed that service, repair, contracts and ongoing support were the aspects customers valued most.

02

From customer friction to opportunity

We mapped the customer journey and identified the main pain points: high upfront investment, difficulty knowing what happens to equipment after use, and lack of a structured way to handle worn or unused gear. These pain points became the foundation for the concept work.

The key insight was that Eleiko was not just selling equipment they could become the provider of a circular service around the equipment lifecycle. That opened a new type of value proposition: access instead of ownership.

03

Concept development: Strength as a Service

One concept focused on a rental and leasing model. Instead of buying equipment outright, customers could access Eleiko equipment through a service agreement, with maintenance, repairs and upgrade cycles included.

Another concept, EleikoCollect, focused on circular recovery: customers could register their old equipment, receive a valuation, and trade it in when upgrading. Eleiko would then refurbish, resell, lease or reuse it, creating a circular business flow.

Concept directions

Concept 01

Strength as a Service

A rental and leasing model where customers access equipment through a service agreement rather than a one-time purchase.

  • Choose between rental or leasing
  • Service and maintenance included
  • Eleiko installs and tracks via product IDs
  • Option to buy out at end of leasing period

Concept 02

EleikoCollect

A circular trade-in service where customers can register used equipment, receive a valuation and trade in when upgrading.

  • Register existing equipment
  • Receive a valuation from Eleiko
  • Trade in when upgrading to new equipment
  • Eleiko refurbishes, resells, leases or recycles
04

Service blueprint and business opportunity

The concepts were framed around a service model rather than only a product or website. That meant thinking about logistics, contracts, inspection, refurbishment and ongoing customer support.

Eleiko would gain better control over the equipment lifecycle, improve sustainability, create a second-life market for premium equipment, and make access easier for customers who could not invest in a full upfront purchase.

Outcome

What I delivered

A service design concept built around circularity, lifecycle control and access to premium equipment. The result was not only a future-facing product idea, but a business model rethink that positioned Eleiko as a brand that could offer service, sustainability and second-life value.

Reflection

What I learned

The biggest learning from Eleiko was that good service design is not only about interfaces; it is about the full lifecycle of an offer. The strongest concept was the one that connected customer value with Eleiko's business reality: premium equipment, long life, circular reuse and service responsibility. That combination made the solution feel both commercially relevant and genuinely sustainable.
